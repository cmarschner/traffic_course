Countries

Role models

Every country faces the same problem: letting people reach what they need without the travel itself doing too much harm. These pages show how seven countries, and several others, answer it. They cover their institutions and standards, the terms of \(W\) they emphasize, their documented successes and failures, and where to find their teaching material, much of it translated here for the first time.

Seven main role models

United States

Origin of the capacity paradigm (HCM, LOS) and the largest experiment in automobile urbanism. Now reforming: VMT instead of LOS, Safe System, congestion pricing.

Terms: A (time) → C

Germany

Dense, codified engineering standards and federal cost–benefit analysis. Cities pursue Verkehrswende: avoid, shift, improve.

Terms: A, C, E · carbon budget

Japan

Accessibility created by rail and density; private railways as city builders; rigorous simulator verification.

Terms: A (access), D

Singapore

Land as the binding constraint. Road pricing since 1975 and a quota on vehicles.

Terms: D · land constraint · pricing

Netherlands

Sustainable Safety and cycling as a mainstream mode; design that makes safe behavior self-evident.

Terms: A, B, C

Sweden

Vision Zero: a constraint, not a price. Two co-equal policy goals of accessibility and safety–environment–health.

Safety constraint · C

France

Statutory socio-economic appraisal with a high carbon value; passive control by geometry (roundabouts, Ville 30).

Terms: C (carbon), E · passive instruments

More countries worth studying

Spain

Pontevedra's car-free center, Barcelona's superblocks, a national 30 km/h urban speed limit.

Italy

Limited-traffic zones, Milan's Area C, Bologna Città 30.

United Kingdom

Empirical capacity models, TAG appraisal, Manual for Streets, low-traffic neighbourhoods.

Denmark

Copenhagen's cycling system and the Finger Plan.

Austria

Vienna's €365 annual pass and the Klimaticket.

Switzerland

Clock-face integrated timetables and the distance-based heavy-vehicle fee.

Colombia & Brazil

Bus rapid transit (Curitiba, Bogotá), cable cars in Medellín, the ciclovía.

South Korea

Removing Seoul's Cheonggyecheon freeway; the 2004 bus reform.

Australia

Safe System, Austroads guides, lane-based junction modelling (SIDRA).

India

Heterogeneous traffic without lane discipline; NPTEL open courses.

China & Hong Kong

Rail-plus-property value capture; three-phase traffic research; electric bus fleets.
How to read the country pages

Characterizations are interpretations of what institutions reward, based on official documents. They are not rankings, and practice varies widely within each country. Each page includes documented criticisms alongside the signature ideas.
