About this course

Edited by Clemens Marschner

Drafted with the help of Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic. Edited and reviewed by the editor, who is responsible for any errors.

Origin and purpose

The course began as a self-study syllabus for building and validating a city-scale traffic simulation. It grew into a broader question: if the simulation is accurate, what should it be used to optimize? National traditions answer that question very differently. This site sets out the answers side by side, around one explicit welfare objective, so that the choice of objective can be discussed openly rather than inherited from a software default.

Editorial principles

  • Primary sources first. Claims about national practice cite the official document (law, guideline or appraisal manual) wherever possible.
  • Interpretations are labeled. Characterizations of national "readings of W" are judgments, not measurements.
  • Balance. Every role model also has documented failures, and the country pages discuss them.
  • Free material preferred. Paid standards (e.g., FGSV guidelines, HCM) are described and cited but not reproduced.

Translation policy

Many of the most instructive documents exist only in Swedish, German, Japanese, French, Dutch, Spanish or Italian. In Sources & translations and throughout the notes:

  • Translations are machine-assisted and reviewed by the editor. They are study aids, not authoritative versions. The original always prevails.
  • Official legal texts (statutes, regulations, parliamentary bills) are translated in full or in large part, since such texts are generally free of copyright restrictions.
  • Copyrighted guidance and teaching material is summarized in English, with only short original passages quoted for study and comment, always with attribution and a link to the original.

Links

External links were checked when the pages were written. Agencies reorganise their websites often. If a link breaks, search for the document title given next to it, or use the Internet Archive.

Technical notes

No build step: the site is hand-written HTML and CSS with a small JavaScript file that renders formulas (via KaTeX) and builds the on-page table of contents. Pages remain readable without JavaScript.
