    Spain's Royal Decree 970/2020: 30 km/h as the national urban default

    Royal Decree · 10 November 2020 · Terms: constraints C A · Modules 11, 12, 7

    In May 2021 Spain became one of the first countries to make 30 km/h the national default speed limit on most urban streets. The rule is simple and geometric: 20 km/h where the carriageway and footway share one level, 30 km/h on streets with one lane per direction, 50 km/h on streets with two or more lanes per direction. Lanes reserved for buses or other specific users do not count. This page translates the new article 50 of the General Traffic Regulation in full and the key reasoning of the preamble.

    Why it matters for W

    Most speed reductions are local decisions, one street or zone at a time, each needing its own justification. Spain inverted the burden of proof: the national default became 30 km/h on single-lane-per-direction streets, and a municipality must take a positive, signed decision to raise it to 50 km/h. In the language of Module 1, this is a move toward treating safety as a constraint: unless a street's geometry separates traffic (two or more lanes each way), it is assumed to be a place where vulnerable road users mix with cars.

    The preamble also makes a claim about Term A that students should test. It argues that lower limits do not significantly slow urban traffic because motor vehicles in city centers generally do not exceed average speeds of about 20 km/h. Whether this holds depends on how much of the trip time is spent at junctions rather than on links, which is the subject of Module 7 and Module 9.

    Digest

    1. Motivation (preamble)

    The preamble describes how the General Traffic Regulation (2003) and the General Vehicle Regulation (1998) were written around the growing use of motor vehicles, which had become "the protagonist" of most of their articles. In cities, the motor vehicle is no longer the protagonist: motorcycles, bicycles, electrically assisted pedal cycles, personal mobility vehicles and pedestrians share the road. Local mobility and safety policies have one main objective, the reduction of crashes in urban areas, and Spanish cities had asked for changes to the general rules so that they could develop new city models.

    The decree gives two facts. In 2017, 509 people died in Spanish cities, and 80 % of them were vulnerable road users (pedestrians, cyclists, motorcyclists and moped riders). And studies agree that the risk of dying when hit by a vehicle is reduced at least five times at 30 km/h compared with 50 km/h. The decree concludes that lowering the generic urban limit to 30 km/h on streets with one lane per direction will reduce casualties, especially among vulnerable users, while keeping traffic moving on the main avenues and reducing the negative effect on urban air quality.

    The preamble also justifies the decree against the principles of good regulation of the Administrative Procedure Act (necessity, effectiveness, proportionality, legal certainty, transparency, efficiency) and records that the draft was put to public consultation and reported on by the Higher Council for Traffic, Road Safety and Sustainable Mobility.

    2. The new speed rule (article 50 RGC)

    The generic urban limits become: 20 km/h on single-platform streets (carriageway and footway at one level), 30 km/h on streets with a single lane per direction, and 50 km/h on streets with two or more lanes per direction. Reserved lanes (for buses, specific users or public transport) are not counted. Municipalities may lower these limits with specific signs, and may exceptionally raise the limit on single-lane-per-direction streets to a maximum of 50 km/h, also with specific signs. Vehicles carrying dangerous goods are limited to 40 km/h on multi-lane urban roads and through-roads (travesías). The generic limit on travesías (inter-urban roads passing through a town) is 50 km/h, and on motorways inside built-up areas 80 km/h. Municipalities and road owners may adopt traffic-calming measures to make the limits perceptible. Breaches are serious offences, or very serious ones under the traffic law where applicable.

    3. Personal mobility vehicles

    The decree defines a personal mobility vehicle (vehículo de movilidad personal, VMP) as a single-seat vehicle with one or more wheels, propelled exclusively by electric motors, with a design speed between 6 and 25 km/h. Seats are allowed only on self-balancing vehicles. VMPs are classified as vehicles, which means they may not use footways or pedestrian zones. They are banned from inter-urban roads, through-roads, urban motorways and urban tunnels. They need a certificate showing compliance with a technical manual issued by the Directorate-General for Traffic (DGT), with a 24-month transition after the manual's publication. Electrically assisted pedal cycles are redefined in line with EU Regulation 168/2013 (motor up to 250 W, assistance cutting out at 25 km/h).

    4. Assessment

    The decree is short and blunt, which is its strength: one national rule, based on street geometry that any driver can see. Its weaknesses are also clear. A posted limit is not a driven speed, and the decree only allows calming measures; it does not fund or require them. Lane-count is an imperfect proxy for function: a wide single-lane arterial without frontage activity gets the same limit as a narrow residential street. Several large cities had already introduced 30 km/h widely before 2021, which complicates any before–after evaluation of the national rule. Students using it as evidence should look for evaluations that control for the general trend in urban casualties and for the COVID-19 period.

    Article 50 RGC · speed limits on urban roads and through-roads, full text

    «Artículo 50. Límites de velocidad en vías urbanas y travesías.
    1. El límite genérico de velocidad en vías urbanas será de:
    a) 20 km/h en vías que dispongan de plataforma única de calzada y acera.
    b) 30 km/h en vías de un único carril por sentido de circulación.
    c) 50 km/h en vías de dos o más carriles por sentido de circulación.
    A estos efectos, los carriles reservados para la circulación de determinados usuarios o uso exclusivo de transporte público no serán contabilizados.
    2. Las velocidades genéricas establecidas podrán ser rebajadas previa señalización específica, por la Autoridad municipal.
    3. Excepcionalmente, la Autoridad Municipal podrá aumentar la velocidad en vías de un único carril por sentido hasta una velocidad máxima de 50 km/h, previa señalización específica.
    4. En las vías urbanas a las que se refiere el apartado 1 c) y en travesías, los vehículos que transporten mercancías peligrosas circularán como máximo a 40 km/h.
    5. El límite genérico de velocidad en travesías es de 50 km/h para todo tipo de vehículos. Este límite podrá ser rebajado por acuerdo de la Autoridad Municipal con el titular de la vía, previa señalización especifica.
    6. El límite genérico de velocidad en autopistas y autovías que transcurren dentro de poblado será de 80 km/h, no obstante podrá ser ampliados por acuerdo de la Autoridad Municipal y el titular de la vía, previa señalización específica, sin rebasar en ningún caso los límites genéricos establecidos para dichas vías fuera de poblado.
    7. Las autoridades municipales y titulares de la vía podrán adoptar las medidas necesarias para lograr el calmado del tráfico y facilitar la percepción de los límites de velocidad establecidos.
    8. Las infracciones a las normas de este precepto tendrán la consideración de graves conforme se prevé en el artículo 76. a), salvo que tengan la consideración de muy graves, de conformidad con lo dispuesto en el artículo 77.a), ambos del texto refundido de la Ley sobre tráfico, circulación de vehículos a motor y seguridad vial.»

    "Article 50. Speed limits on urban roads and through-roads.
    1. The generic speed limit on urban roads shall be:
    (a) 20 km/h on roads with a single platform for carriageway and footway.
    (b) 30 km/h on roads with a single lane in each direction of travel.
    (c) 50 km/h on roads with two or more lanes in each direction of travel.
    For these purposes, lanes reserved for certain users or for the exclusive use of public transport shall not be counted.
    2. The generic speeds established may be lowered by the municipal authority, with specific signs.
    3. Exceptionally, the municipal authority may raise the speed on roads with a single lane in each direction up to a maximum of 50 km/h, with specific signs.
    4. On the urban roads referred to in paragraph 1(c) and on through-roads, vehicles carrying dangerous goods shall travel at no more than 40 km/h.
    5. The generic speed limit on through-roads is 50 km/h for all types of vehicle. This limit may be lowered by agreement between the municipal authority and the road owner, with specific signs.
    6. The generic speed limit on motorways and dual carriageways running within built-up areas shall be 80 km/h; it may however be raised by agreement between the municipal authority and the road owner, with specific signs, without in any case exceeding the generic limits set for such roads outside built-up areas.
    7. Municipal authorities and road owners may adopt the measures necessary to achieve traffic calming and to make the established speed limits easier to perceive.
    8. Breaches of the rules in this article shall be considered serious offences as provided in article 76(a), unless they are considered very serious in accordance with article 77(a), both of the consolidated text of the Law on Traffic, Motor Vehicle Circulation and Road Safety."
