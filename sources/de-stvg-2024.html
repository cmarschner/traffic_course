    Germany's 2024 road traffic law reform: new aims in StVG § 6 and StVO § 45

    Federal statute and regulation · 2024 · Terms: C A D · Modules 12, 6, 13, 11

    For decades, German road traffic law allowed authorities to restrict traffic essentially only to avert danger to the "safety or ease" (Sicherheit oder Leichtigkeit) of traffic. In 2024 the legislature added climate protection, health and urban development as legitimate aims for a defined set of measures: bus lanes, space for cycling and walking, residents' parking and pilot schemes. This page translates the new provisions in full and explains how they change what German municipalities may optimize.

    Why it matters for W

    German street-space decisions are made under two different legal regimes. Road law (Straßenrecht, federal and state road acts) decides what a street is for and how it is built. Road traffic law (Straßenverkehrsrecht, StVG and StVO) decides how existing space may be used: speed limits, lane allocation, parking. Before 2024, traffic orders under the StVO had to be justified almost entirely by danger to safety or traffic flow. In the notation of this course, an authority could act on part of Term A (traffic flow) and on the safety constraint, but not on Term C (climate, health) or Term D (urban development and land use). Courts regularly annulled bus lanes, cycle lanes or 30 km/h limits because no specific danger had been shown.

    The 2024 reform does three things that map directly onto \(W\):

    • It makes climate, environment and health legitimate aims for certain orders (Term C).
    • It makes support for orderly urban development a legitimate aim (Terms D and A, via the opportunity term \(\ln O_j\)).
    • It keeps two side conditions: the "ease of traffic" must be taken into account, and safety must not be impaired. In course terms, safety stays a constraint, and motor-traffic flow is a consideration to be weighed, not a veto.

    The reform is modest in scope. It does not allow a general 30 km/h limit, and it does not apply to every kind of order. It is nevertheless a clear example of a legislature rewriting the objective function that traffic engineers are legally allowed to use.

    Digest

    1. The old logic: danger prevention

    § 6(1) StVG empowers the Federal Ministry to issue regulations "in so far as necessary to avert dangers to the safety or ease of traffic on public roads". The StVO, issued under that power, lets road-traffic authorities restrict or prohibit the use of roads "for reasons of the safety or order of traffic" (§ 45(1) sentence 1). § 45(9) adds a restrictive rule: traffic signs may only be ordered where "compellingly necessary", and restrictions on moving traffic only where local conditions create a danger that "considerably exceeds" the general risk. This "qualified danger" test was the main legal obstacle to Tempo 30 on main roads and to reallocating lanes.

    Before 2024 there were already exceptions: protection of residents from noise and exhaust (§ 45(1) sentence 2 no. 3), Tempo 30 zones in residential areas (§ 45(1c)), cycle streets, cycle lanes, pedestrian crossings and others listed in § 45(9). Environmental zones were ordered under air-quality plans.

    2. What the 2024 StVG amendment changed

    The Tenth Act amending the StVG made four substantive changes to § 6:

    1. It inserted a new paragraph (4a). Regulations on traffic behavior (no. 2), on measures against nuisance and environmental harm or to support urban development (no. 8), on residents' parking and pilot schemes (no. 15 b and c), on special lanes (no. 16) and on general exemptions for trials (no. 18) may now also be issued to improve environmental protection, including climate protection, to protect health, or to support urban development. Such regulations should allow municipalities to apply to the competent authority for orders on all these grounds. Orders must take the ease of traffic into account and must not impair safety.
    2. It widened residents' parking (no. 15 b): parking shortage may now be either proven or expected on the basis of concrete urban and transport planning considerations.
    3. It widened special lanes (no. 16) to the testing of new forms of mobility and the reduction of the number of trips.
    4. It required that regulations under the new paragraph be issued jointly by the transport, environment and economy/climate ministries.

    3. What the 2024 StVO amendment changed

    The ordinance used the new power. The key provision is § 45(1) sentence 2 no. 7: authorities may now restrict or regulate traffic to improve environmental and climate protection, to protect health, or to support orderly urban development, for two purposes: (a) bus lanes and signal priority for buses, and (b) the provision of adequate space for moving and parked bicycles and for walking. Orders under no. 7 are exempt from the qualified-danger test of § 45(9) (see § 45(10) no. 2).

    The list of measures exempt from the qualified-danger test in § 45(9) now includes, among others, short 30 km/h sections of up to 500 m between two 30 km/h sections, and 30 km/h on main roads in the immediate vicinity of pedestrian crossings, kindergartens, playgrounds, busy school routes, schools, care homes, facilities for people with disabilities and hospitals. Federal-government and UBA summaries describe the gap-closing rule and the additions of pedestrian crossings, playgrounds and busy school routes as part of the 2024 package. Bus lanes (sign 245) and pedestrian crossings (sign 293) are also listed. Under § 45(1j), municipalities may apply to the competent authority for orders under paragraphs (1) to (1i).

    4. What did not change

    • No general urban speed limit change. The statutory urban limit remains 50 km/h. A coalition of several hundred municipalities had asked for freedom to set 30 km/h where they see fit; the 2024 reform gives them more cases, not a general power.
    • Safety remains a hard side condition. Every order under the new aims must not impair safety. In \(W\), the safety constraint still binds.
    • Traffic flow is still weighed. "Ease of traffic" must be considered. Authorities therefore still need to show they weighed delay to motor traffic. That is close to the logsum view: vehicle delay is part of A, but not the whole of A.
    • The road-traffic authority decides. Municipalities can apply, but in many states the decision rests with a traffic authority that may be a different level of government.

    5. Reading the reform through W

    Consider a four-lane urban arterial with a proposed bus lane that takes one general lane in each direction. Before 2024, the authority had to argue that the bus lane averted a specific danger or improved the "order" of traffic. A model showing more car delay was often decisive. After 2024, the authority can justify the order by lower CO₂ and better air quality (Term C), by more people moved per lane (Term A, counted in persons, not vehicles) and by support for planned development along the corridor (Term D). It must still show that car delay was weighed and that safety does not suffer. This is a move from the classic objective \(\min\sum q_a t_a\) toward a multi-term objective, which is exactly the shift Module 1 describes.

    § 6(4a) StVG · new aims (inserted 2024), full text

    (4a) Rechtsverordnungen nach Absatz 1 Satz 1 Nummer 2, 8, 15 Buchstabe b oder c, Nummer 16 oder 18 können auch erlassen werden zur Verbesserung des Schutzes der Umwelt, darunter des Klimaschutzes, zum Schutz der Gesundheit oder zur Unterstützung der städtebaulichen Entwicklung, soweit sie nicht bereits nach Absatz 4 erlassen werden können. Diese Rechtsverordnungen sollen insbesondere vorsehen, dass Gemeinden bei den nach Landesrecht für die Ausführung der Rechtsverordnungen bestimmten Behörden den Erlass von Anordnungen zur Gewährleistung der Sicherheit und Leichtigkeit des Verkehrs, zur Verbesserung des Schutzes der Umwelt, zum Schutz der Gesundheit oder zur Unterstützung der städtebaulichen Entwicklung beantragen können. Die nach Satz 1 erlassenen Rechtsverordnungen und auf ihnen beruhenden Anordnungen müssen neben der Verbesserung des Schutzes der Umwelt, des Schutzes der Gesundheit oder der Unterstützung der städtebaulichen Entwicklung die Leichtigkeit des Verkehrs berücksichtigen und dürfen die Sicherheit des Verkehrs nicht beeinträchtigen.

    (4a) Regulations under paragraph (1), sentence 1, numbers 2, 8, 15(b) or (c), 16 or 18 may also be issued to improve the protection of the environment, including climate protection, to protect health, or to support urban development, in so far as they cannot already be issued under paragraph (4). These regulations shall in particular provide that municipalities may apply to the authorities designated under state law to implement the regulations for orders to ensure the safety and ease of traffic, to improve the protection of the environment, to protect health or to support urban development. Regulations issued under sentence 1, and orders based on them, must, in addition to improving the protection of the environment, protecting health or supporting urban development, take into account the ease of traffic and must not impair the safety of traffic.
