# Tenets

The principles the course is written by. Change requests (`change-requests.md`) are checked
against them.

## Who it is for

0. **Empower the people who decide, and the citizens who hold them to account.** Readers in
   order of priority: decision makers and policy makers; urban and transport planners;
   informed citizens. Lead with what a decision maker needs; keep the method in a layer
   planners can open and others can skip. See `audience.md`.

## What the course argues

1. **Transport exists to improve the welfare of the people who use it.** Trips are means.
   Access to a good life (jobs, education, care, friendship, choice) is the end.
2. **It must do so while maximizing overall welfare,** minimizing the harm travel does to
   everyone else, spread in space (neighbors, children, communities, climate) and in time
   (accumulating emissions, paved land, locked-in city shape).
3. **Make the objective explicit.** One welfare function W = A + B − C − D − E with limits
   (deaths, carbon, land, minimum access). Every model, appraisal and national practice is
   read as a choice of terms, prices, weights and limits.
4. **Count every effect once,** in the place where the person who bears it is counted.

## How it is written

5. **Start from places and people,** not from formulas. Stories, history, portraits and
   visuals first; the formula last, after the motivation.
6. **Introduce before use; at most one new idea per sentence.** Define jargon on first use.
   Spell out steps that look obvious to mathematicians. Say why a step is taken before taking it.
7. **Write for an external audience.** No "the familiar form". Drop terms that do not matter.
8. **Be fair to every tradition.** Each role model has documented failures; each "bad"
   example had real gains. Interpretations are labeled as judgments.
9. **Primary sources first,** translated where needed. Paid standards are described and cited,
   not reproduced.
10. **Only freely licensed material,** credited on the page and in `about.html`.
11. **US English, mobile-readable, works without JavaScript** (scripts only enhance).
